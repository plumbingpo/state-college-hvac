When Your System Fails in Pittsburgh

When your system quits, how fast help shows up matters more than anything else. That's why we keep scheduling flexible across the area and answer every call with a live person. Urgent calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat a failed furnace in a cold snap or a dead AC in the heat ahead of routine work.

The urgent calls we run tend to follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's warm air from the vents, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil frozen solid. On the heating side, it's a furnace that ignites and drops out. And any time of year, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, clogged condensate drains backing water into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have quit. Since our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, most of these get fixed on the first trip, at a price you approve before we start.

A little caution while you wait for us can prevent a much bigger repair. An iced-over AC should be turned off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us right away.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Pittsburgh

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners last 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or a service call every season, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

Minor issues rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but left alone for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a miserable night.
